Flat, straight-on scan of the BACK of a premium poker-size playing card, filling the whole image edge to edge (no table, no shadow, no perspective, no hands, no other cards). Classic layout exactly like a Bicycle Rider Back or a US Playing Card Co. engraved back: a thin plain cream margin border all around (about 4% of the width), then a framed rectangular panel with a fine double-line frame, filled with a dense, perfectly symmetrical ornamental pattern that has true 180-degree rotational symmetry (the top half is the bottom half turned upside down) and left-right mirror symmetry. Fine engraved, banknote-style guilloché linework, crisp vector-clean lines, consistent line weights, no blur, no mushy texture, no noise. The aviation theme is SUBTLE, discovered on a second look: rosettes whose petals are three-blade propeller blades, acanthus-like scroll leaves shaped as swept-back wings, tiny engraved biplanes and small jet silhouettes hidden inside the filigree corners, scrollwork that curls like contrails, a border row of rivet dots, small runway chevrons, altimeter-style tick rings around the medallion. From a distance it must read as a traditional elegant playing card back. No text anywhere, no letters, no numbers.
Colour: three inks on cream paper #f4ecd8: deep navy #0d2238 for the main engraved linework, soft sky blue #4aa8dc for a fine guilloché wave tint and the outer frame band, warm orange #e8812e as a sparse accent. Design: a banknote-engraved airline-ticket-style back. The panel background is a fine sky-blue moiré of concentric wavy guilloché lines like the security tint of a vintage airline ticket. A sky-blue frame band with a row of navy rivet dots. Over the tint, dense navy-engraved acanthus scrolls whose leaves are clearly swept-back aircraft wings with feather-like ribs, curling into contrail spirals. Central circular medallion: an outer ring of plain altimeter tick marks with an orange hairline ring, inside it a large engraved PROPELLER rosette: a four-blade aircraft propeller seen head-on, long narrow twisted aerofoil blades with a pointed spinner hub, layered over a second four-blade propeller rotated 45 degrees, behind it fine spirograph guilloché rings (it must read as propellers, not a flower). Four smaller matching propeller-roundels in the corners. Small orange runway chevrons at the top and bottom midpoints pointing to the centre. Tiny jet silhouettes hidden inside two of the scroll curls.
Strict rules: square corners, the cream margin runs to the very edge of the image; absolutely no numerals, letters or degree markings on any dial or ring, only plain tick marks; every hidden aircraft is a clean tiny silhouette; the design must be crisp at full size and still read as a bold clear pattern when shrunk to a 60-pixel thumbnail.
