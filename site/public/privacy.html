Sky Collector

Privacy

Last updated 20 November 2026 · This covers the test version at sevaan.github.io/sky-collector and the sample pack at skycollector.app/pack. It will be rewritten before the game is in the app stores.

The short version: your location is used to find the planes over you. There are no accounts. Your collection stays on your device. There are no ads, no tracking and no selling of data; the game sends a few anonymous usage notes, which you can turn off.

Your location

Sky Collector needs to know where you are to show the planes in your sky. If you allow precise location, your phone uses it to draw each plane where it really is (a plane 10 km away can be drawn several degrees off from an approximate position). Your precise position never leaves your device. Everything that is sent is rounded first, and it is used for two things only:

  • Planes near you, in the apps. The iPhone and Android apps ask adsb.lol directly from your phone for aircraft around a position rounded to a 0.05-degree grid (about 5 km), and switch to Sky Collector's own relay (which asks Fly Italy ADSB) with the same rounded position if adsb.lol is busy or your phone has used its daily allowance. The search circle is made a little bigger so the rounding never hides a plane. Aircraft data: adsb.lol contributors, under the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0.
  • Planes near you, on the website. The page asks Sky Collector's own server for aircraft around the same rounded position. The server asks Fly Italy ADSB for the whole 1-degree square around it (only the square's centre is sent onward), keeps the answer for under a minute, and keeps no record of who asked. Aircraft data: Fly Italy ADSB, CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • The weather. The forecast for your area comes from Open-Meteo, asked with a position rounded to 0.1 degree (about 10 km).

The anonymous usage notes described below never contain a location. If you choose "Approximate" location, the game still works; planes may be drawn a little off, and Settings says so.

Like any website, each of those services sees your internet address when it is asked. You can choose a place by hand in Settings, and the game works without your location (it shows an example sky).

The sample pack page

The sample pack at skycollector.app/pack, which players send their friends, never asks for your location. To put a real flight in the pack, the page reads your device's time zone (for example "America/Toronto"), picks that time zone's big city and asks Sky Collector's own plane server for the aircraft near the city's centre. Only the city's coordinates are sent, never yours. The server asks the community flight-tracking network FlyItalyADSB about the centre of a 1-degree square around that city, never the city or you. Like any website, Sky Collector's server sees your internet address when it is asked. If it doesn't answer in time, the pack shows a flight the server saw over a nearby city earlier, with the day it was seen. Nothing from the pack is kept.

Your collection

Your cards, catches, patches and progress are stored in your browser on your device. Each catch records the time, the aircraft and where you were standing, because that is part of the card. There is no sign-up and no account, and nothing of your collection is sent anywhere. Clearing this site's data in your browser deletes it.

Sky Collector also keeps a tally on your device of the aircraft types that have flown over you in the last 30 days. It is how the game knows what is rare where you are. It never leaves your device.

The aircraft you see

Aircraft broadcast their position publicly, and volunteers with receivers share those broadcasts. Sky Collector shows the airline, the route and the type of an airline flight. Every other aircraft is anonymous in the game: private and business planes (including fractional and charter business jets), helicopters, and any aircraft whose owner has asked for it not to be tracked publicly (the FAA's LADD and PIA programmes). For those Sky Collector shows the type only. Military, police, coast guard and other government aircraft are shown by their type only, such as a C-17 or a B-2, and only when they broadcast their position: never by callsign, registration, unit or route, and they never get a flight card. It never shows or keeps a registration, a callsign or an owner, and the aircraft's transponder code is replaced on your device by a scrambled number that only lets your card count different aircraft. Nothing in the game rewards following one aircraft or person.

Routes come from Virtual Radar Server's standing data (CC0), bundled with the game. Aircraft photos and airline logos come from Wikimedia Commons; each photo's author and licence is shown on its card. Airline names and logos belong to their owners and are used only to say which airline a flight is.

Usage and error reports

To learn whether the game works for people (does a first day end with a card? do people come back?), Sky Collector (the apps and this website) sends a few anonymous notes: when the app is opened, each day you catch something, how long a session lasted, what kind of aircraft each catch was (an airliner, a private plane, a helicopter, a balloon and so on) and whether it gave you a new card, whether the phone's motion sensors work, the tour finished or skipped, how the plane feed did in that session (how many times it asked for planes and whether the free feed's daily limit was reached), and errors that stop something working. They're tied to a random ID made on your phone, not to you: no location, no name, no device identifier, nothing from your contacts or photos, and nothing that identifies an aircraft (no callsign, registration or transponder code). They're kept on our server (Google Cloud, in Sky Collector's own project) and used only to improve the game. You can turn them off any time in Settings → Help → Share anonymous usage. The test website's notes are marked as tests and left out of the counts.

What this version does not do

  • No ads and no advertising trackers.
  • No advertising or third-party analytics; only the anonymous usage notes above, which you can turn off.
  • No purchases: Sky Collector Plus is not on sale in the test version.
  • No notifications sent from a server, and no shared-card links.
  • It never sells or shares your data.

Children

Sky Collector is made for everyone, including families. There are no accounts, and it collects no personal information from anyone, children included.
