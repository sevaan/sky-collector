Design spike · 2026-11-21 · Collection header

Sky's second card

Space Collector's Collection opens on a two-card deck: your rank and a live globe of your satellites. Planes have no orbits, so Sky needs something else behind (or in front of) the rank card. Three takes, each drawn at the real size of the header card on a 375px phone.

Card: 335 × 314 px, radius 16, cream paper with grain, 36 px label strip, 196 px picture, Russo One headline. Same sample player throughout: 23 passes · 13 airlines · 31 airports in 14 countries. Deck: the card behind sits 14 px higher at 94% and 55% brightness, as css/collection.css does now.

TAKE 1Passport

A passport page that gets an ink stamp for every airport your passes touch, in mixed shapes, inks and tilts, with the newest stamp thumping down and a dashed slot waiting for the next. It's Sevaan's idea, and it gives every boarding pass a second reward: a new airport is a new stamp.

Uses: each pass's from/to IATA code, city and country, the date you caught it (first visit stamps the airport), rarity of that pass (gold ink for a gold pass). 8 stamps a page; the strip of country codes fills in underneath.

TAKE 2Route map

A top-of-the-world map with a great-circle arc for every pass you've caught, airports as dots, gold passes in gold and your longest route drawn bold with a little jet flying it. Seen from over the pole, the routes curve the way they really fly, so a Toronto to Seoul pass arcs over the Arctic instead of crossing a flat Pacific.

Uses: each pass's from/to airport coordinates (from the airport table the boarding pass already uses), rarity, and the great-circle distance for the longest route. No location of the player: the map shows airports and routes only, never where you were standing.

TAKE 5 · MY PICKTail wall

A hangar wall of airline tail fins: every airline you've caught stands on the shelf in its real livery, with how many passes you have from it, and the ones still to find stand as grey silhouettes. Under it, three meters fill up toward the alliance sets (Star Alliance, SkyTeam, oneworld), so there's always a next airline to look up for.

Uses: the airline on each pass (assets/logos/tails/, already in the repo, the same art the radar strip uses), a count per airline, and a small airline → alliance table. Airline flights only: military, government and private planes never appear here.

Recommendation

Lead with the Passport, and keep the Tail wall as its back page. The passport is the strongest second card for the deck: it reads as a collection at a glance (stamps are a universal “I've been there”), every new airport is a visible reward on the card you see most, and it ties straight into the boarding pass, which already prints the codes. The empty slot and the 14 country codes give an obvious "one more" at every size.

The Tail wall is my own idea and the most game-like (named sets, silhouettes to fill, art already in the repo), but it rewards airlines, which the passes already show big; it works best as the passport's flip side or its own Collection tab. The Route map is the prettiest at day one, but the arcs pile up over Europe and the north-east US after a few dozen passes, and the stylised continents would need real work (or a proper outline file) to ship. Note for all three: behind the rank card only the 14 px label strip shows, and at 55% brightness Sky's cream paper turns grey; a lighter dim (about 85%) is shown under Take 1.
