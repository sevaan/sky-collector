Four new layouts, each holding every field on the front: set, type code, rarity, photo and credit, maker and model, kind, four specs, the airline and registration of this catch, your catch (when, height, speed, distance, heading), progress (2 of 10 different aircraft for Silver, Bronze, 2 liveries) and the fact. Gallery

Photo on top, the name, then a two-column ledger: the type on the left, your catch on the right, each four plain rows with no boxes. Progress is one tinted strip with the medal; the fact closes the card under a hairline. The calmest, most "museum" of the four.

2

Spine

A navy spine down the left holds every number: the type code at the top, the four specs, the four catch numbers, the medal at the foot. The rest is photo, name and the story of this catch (when, the airline, how far along you are), then the fact. Strongest shape at a glance.

3 ★

Ticket

The card is the type; a tear-off stub is your catch. Above the perforation: photo, name, the stat strip, the fact. Below, on pale sky paper: when, the airline and its registration as a plate, the four numbers of what you saw, and your progress. It pairs with the boarding pass's stub on the flight card.

Collection tile · photo, maker, model, rarity, and the stub: catch date and medal
4

Instruments

Every number drawn. The specs as small diagrams (a timeline, two engine fans, the range as a trip toward the far side of the Earth, the wingspan measured); your catch as an altimeter, a speed arc, radar rings and a compass; progress as ten pips. The most game-like and fun to read, the busiest.

Collection tile · photo, maker, model, rarity, the ten pips
3 ★

Ticket, Rare

The favourite as a Rare. The frame turns to gold, the chip gets the gold diamond and Rare's blue ink, a foil streak crosses the photo over a gold hairline, and the stub's paper warms from sky blue to gold. Nothing else moves, so the rarity reads instantly without changing the layout.
