The boarding pass, landscape

The pass becomes a real boarding-pass rectangle: 42em wide (the plane card's height) by 16em tall, 2.625 : 1. Same paper as today's pass: warm stock, grain and fibres, a red airline rule, a dashed tear line with notches cut through, and the stub on the right end. Everything below is drawn at true proportions with the real fonts, logo and photo. The plane card in the phone frames is a screenshot of the real card.

1 · Two layouts

L1: photo on the left, the route and numbers to its right, an Aztec code (the 2D code real passes carry) on the stub. L2: the photo as a wide band across the top, route and numbers in one row under it, a barcode running down the stub.

2 · The two-card reveal, portrait

The plane card in front at its usual size (300 px wide on a 375 px phone). Two ways to stand the pass behind it, then the pass brought forward.

3 · Turned sideways

Turn the phone and the pass fills the screen, the right way up, at about 1.6 times the size the plane card is drawn at.

4 · Collection, Passes tab

Passes as tickets instead of the portrait grid: one per row reads like a stack of real passes; two per row fits twice as many on screen.
