Generic plane art

The plane card's picture when Sky doesn't know the exact type, or has no photo for it. Today it's the flat jet glyph on a sky panel, the same jet for Heavy, Large, Small and Fast. Three directions below, every size class in each. The photo windows are drawn at their real size on a 375 px phone (297 × 200), and the cards are the app's own plane cards with only the picture swapped.

Today (the Collection minis)
A

Travel poster

Each class drawn side-on like an old airline poster: cream aircraft in navy line, red tail and cheatline, a low sun behind, flat cloud banks and a contrail. Heavy gets the four-wheel bogie and big engines, Large the winglets, Small a T-tailed bizjet. Unidentified ducks into a cloud.

B

Blueprint

A spotter's sheet on drafting-blue paper: the outline from below (what you see from the ground) and side-on, white line, the span and length dimensioned, and a title block with the class. It belongs with the plane card's spec-sheet face. Unidentified is drawn dashed with a question mark.

C

Real sky

A photo without a photo: a layered daytime sky with sun glare, cirrus and cumulus, and the aircraft small and high, seen from below the way you really see it, with contrails behind jets. Low things (helicopter, balloon, drone) come in side-on and nearer. Unidentified is just a distant speck and its contrail.

Recommendation

A, travel poster. It reads as art rather than an icon, it is the most distinct class to class at mini size (side-on shows gear, engines, tails, rotors, the basket), it stays in the app's daytime palette, and it sits well on the spec sheet. B is the closest match to the card's drafting face but the blue panel is heavy on a cream card and plan views of Heavy, Large and Small look alike when small. C is the most "true to life", but the subject is small by design, so at mini size it leans on the outline alone, and its blur filters cost the most to draw in a grid.
